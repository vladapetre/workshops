# Component Principles in Clean Architecture

## Introduction

Component Principles in Clean Architecture provide foundational guidance for structuring software systems at a higher level of abstraction than classes or methods. While SOLID principles address the internal design of classes and objects, component principles focus on the relationships, dependencies, and organization of larger building blocks—**components, modules, or packages**—within a system.

These principles are essential for achieving **modularity**, **scalability**, and **maintainability** in complex applications. By applying component principles, architects can ensure that the system is easy to evolve, test, and deploy, while minimizing the risks associated with tight coupling and poor separation of concerns.

Component principles are typically grouped into three categories:

- **Cohesion Principles:** Guide how classes and modules should be grouped into components.
- **Coupling Principles:** Define how components should depend on each other.
- **Substitutability Principles:** Ensure components can be replaced or reused without impacting the system’s integrity.

Understanding and applying these principles is crucial for building robust, adaptable architectures that can withstand change and growth over time.
